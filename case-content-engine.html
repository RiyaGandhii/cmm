ONE SHOOT DAY. SIX formats.[Content operations · SEO · Scripting]

[Brief]
A lean team cannot out-post bigger brands, so every shoot had to do several jobs.
[My role]
Designed the system and ran it: calendar, scripts, shoot batching, repurposing, SEO, approvals and publishing.
[Result]
4 long-form + 26 Shorts + 1 podcast a month, from batched shoots.

I built a system where one shoot day with Dr. Sarkar feeds every format the channel runs. It rests on three habits: a standard script structure, batched shoots, and a repurposing pass that turns each recording into several pieces.

The result is a steady monthly cadence from a small team, with search built into every piece.

[1/3]

[01]
The scripting standard: TSYS

Long-form videos were losing viewers at around the 50-second mark: the reason to care arrived too late. I built TSYS (Tell, Show, Yield, Summarize), a context-first structure that uses short teasers to front-load why the video matters. It became the channel’s long-form scripting standard.

  1. Tell: say why it matters, up front.
  2. Show: give the visual proof.
  3. Yield: deliver the answer.
  4. Summarize: leave one thing to remember.

[02]
One recording, many pieces, all searchable

Shoots are batched around the doctor’s time, and every podcast and long-form recording is cut for Shorts. I pull the moments from the transcript, so the clip list is ready before the edit starts. Every piece then ships with an SEO package: title options, a description, hashtags, search terms and tags, written around how people actually search.

[03]
Beyond the channel

I scripted Instagram Shorts for creator partners, wrote vox pop question sets, and onboarded new doctors onto the shoot calendar, so the brand added voices without adding a new production process.

[04]
The workflow

The editor cuts, the creative manager reviews, and I approve and publish. I owned the calendar, scripts, deadlines and final sign-off, and wrote the onboarding guide for new editors.

A lean team can’t out-post bigger brands. It can out-plan them.

[What I’d do next]
