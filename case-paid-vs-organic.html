WHICH RESULTS WERE really OURS?[Measurement · Attribution · Reporting]

[Brief]
Show what content earned on its own, and connect it to the business.
[My role]
Built the reporting from YouTube Studio and store data, set the measurement rules the plan ran on, and presented performance and budget summaries to the founder.
[Result]
About 3x YouTube-referred store sessions, year on year.

Total views can hide the story. Paid ad campaigns can account for most of a channel’s views, and they say very little about what the content earns by itself.

I built the reporting that separates paid from organic, then went a step further and traced content toward the store, so every plan started from what the content actually earned.

[01]
How I split it

I export video-level data from YouTube Studio, tag the videos that were ad campaigns, and rebuild every total for organic content only. Then I read the trends by format: long-form, Shorts and podcast.

[02]
From views to the store

Views are not the goal. I compared YouTube-referred store sessions year on year in Shopify, and they had grown about 3x. I also found that purchases completing in a separate checkout were invisible to the dashboard, so the reported conversion rate badly understated what content was driving. I recommended UTM links on every video so content could get credit for the sales it brings in.

Blended numbers flatter the team and mislead the plan.

[Impact]
About 3x[YouTube-referred store sessions, year on year.]
14M+[organic views in 13 months.]
73K+[organic watch hours in 13 months.]

Sources: YouTube Studio export and Shopify. Paid and organic are reported separately.

[What I’d do next]

UTM links on every video from day one, and a monthly content-to-revenue report that sits next to the views report, not behind it.
