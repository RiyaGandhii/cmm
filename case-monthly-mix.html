[CASE 01 / 04]

4 LONG VIDEOS. 26 Shorts. ONE PLAN.[Content strategy · Planning · Analytics]

[Brief]
Grow an audience the brand owns, instead of renting reach with ads.
[My role]
Owned the content plan end to end: strategy, scripts, shoot schedule, SEO, reporting, and the budget across ads, creator Shorts and production.
[Result]
49.2K to 100K subscribers in 19 months.

Before the reset, the channel published six long-form videos a month and leaned on ads for reach. Subscribers kept climbing, but the audience data showed retention slipping.

I rebuilt the monthly plan around what the data rewarded: four long-form videos and twenty-six Shorts, with the podcast recorded on the long-form shoot day. Fewer, better pieces, and a clear job for every format.

[1/2]

[01]
What the data said

Ad-driven views were inflating the totals. Once paid and organic were read separately, the pattern was clear: long-form earned the watch time, and Shorts brought in the subscribers. So I stopped treating every piece of content as equal and gave each format one job.

[02]
How I sold it internally

Cutting output sounds like doing less. I made the case with the brand’s own data: which formats earned attention without ad support, and what each one was for. The plan was approved and became the monthly model.

Fewer, better pieces. A clear job for every format.

[Impact]

The channel over the same period.

49.2K to 100K[subscribers in 19 months.]
7 months in a row[above 1M organic views (Oct 2025 to Apr 2026).]

Source: YouTube Studio export. Subscriber growth is channel-wide. Organic views exclude paid ad campaigns.

[What I’d do next]

Give every format a business goal as well as a platform goal: long-form for search and consultations, Shorts for new audiences, the podcast for loyalty, each with its own number.
